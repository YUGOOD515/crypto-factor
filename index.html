01 / 04

Eight factors, one format

Every factor is a weekly return on a shared calendar. Weeks run Monday to Sunday: signals are measured on the formation Sunday and returns are earned through the following Sunday. Each factor is drawn as its data fingerprint, generated from its own return statistics.

Two data sources

Six factors use trading data: price, market capitalization and volume from CoinMarketCap. The two value factors (M007) use on-chain activity, namely weekly active addresses from Glassnode or Dune. The fingerprints follow suit: cool hues for trading data, warm hues for on-chain data.

03/ 06

Factor data · Methodology

Built the same way, every week.

All factors share one calendar, one investable universe and one weighting rule. They differ only in the signal and the sort.

  1. 01Raw dataPrice, market cap and volume, active addresses, risk-free rate
  2. 02Investable universeSeven rules, including a price-consistency filter
  3. 03SignalOne per factor, measured on the formation Sunday
  4. 04SortBreakpoints: 30 / 70, 2 × 3 or deciles
  5. 05PortfoliosWeighted by formation-day market cap
  6. 06Factor returnLong leg minus short leg, next week

3.1

Weekly calendar

Each formation week runs Monday to Sunday and is labelled by its Sunday, formation_date. The return is earned from that Sunday's close to the next Sunday's close, return_date.

1Returns are decimals rounded to four places: 0.0125 is 1.25% per week. A blank cell is missing, not zero.

2Except MKT, every factor is a portfolio spread, so no risk-free rate is subtracted.

3.2

Investable universe

A coin enters a week's cross-section only if it passes all seven rules:

  1. Not on the stablecoin list.
  2. Formation-day closing price above zero.
  3. Formation-day volume above zero.
  4. Formation-day market capitalization strictly above US$1,000,000.
  5. Next-week return exists and is finite.
  6. No identified coin-lifecycle break is crossed by the weekly return or by any historical signal.
  7. Passes the paper-style CoinMarketCap price-consistency filter.

Price-consistency filter

Each coin-day needs a price, a market cap and a volume. A day counts as inconsistent when:

  • volume is zero but price or market cap changes;
  • volume is non-zero but price or market cap stays exactly unchanged;
  • price and market cap are both zero on two consecutive days;
  • price, market cap or volume is missing.

3The filter flag filt equals 1 only when the price, close and market-cap windows are all complete.
